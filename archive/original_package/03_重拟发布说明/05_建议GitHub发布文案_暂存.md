## Overview

DNO is an experimental, trainable bottom-layer neural generative architecture built around evolving effective state, reusable interaction dynamics, recursively participating relationship carriers, and state-dependent future behavior. It is designed for research and subsequent training across different tasks and modalities, not as a ready-made commercial chat model.

## Validated achievements

- **162/162 executable tests passed** in the prescribed code, including core dynamics (32), multi-coordinate research (114), and cross-domain numeric input contract (16).
- **Four synthetic-task checkpoints reloaded and retested** on fresh generated examples; successful controlled pair addressing and one-bit delayed-history retention.
- **64-seed counterfactual study** showed retained downstream state differences after actual interactions; relationship carriers changed future addressing when present.
- **160-seed observational stress study** demonstrated complementary views of values, local rules, boundaries, links, identities, and time; observational blind spots are documented, not conflated with core failures.
- **Frozen core integrity** verified: `dno.py` SHA-256 `fea8d9ea81c94dbf926b984b3c158f301afc71782f989c73b3c9bd02fac767b1`.

## Research direction and expected effects

Expanded training, compute, feedback, longer-horizon tasks and like-for-like comparisons with LLMs, Transformers, recurrent and state-space architectures may reveal new behavior in learned structural composition, persistent effective state, dynamic relationships, streaming signals, and cross-coordinate interpretation. These are research hypotheses to test and document rather than assertions of measured superiority or predefined limits on capability.

## Distribution scope

The package preserves the **72-file frozen execution distribution** (including four model checkpoints and all existing evidence) byte-for-byte and separately archives historical research. Revised explanatory texts are isolated from the generating kernel. Numeric image/audio/video examples demonstrate the common input contract; expected task effects require dedicated training and matched baselines.
